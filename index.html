Where model design met policy constraints — including what was rejected and why.

01

Why empirical PD over structural approaches

Model Design MRM

For the commercial lending portfolios I've worked on, empirical PD models (logistic regression, gradient boosting) were chosen over structural models (Merton-family) because we lacked reliable market-value proxies for private borrowers' assets. Structural models require observable equity or asset volatility — unavailable for SME and thin-file segments. Empirical models trained on behavioural and transaction data outperform on discrimination in these segments. The trade-off is acknowledged: empirical models are weaker at capturing macroeconomic regime shifts, which is why we layer stress-testing scenarios as a separate governance control rather than relying on the PD model alone for downturn estimation.

Reasoning — Structural requires market observables we don't have. Empirical + stress overlay is the pragmatic architecture.
02

FFT-based features: what worked and what didn't

Feature Engineering Scipy

Standard aggregation (mean/sum) of merchant deposit data collapses businesses with different cash-flow cycles into the same feature space. Applying FFT to extract dominant periodicity allowed normalizing RFM metrics by business cycle rather than calendar month. This became a top-5 predictive variable. What didn't work: the initial implementation was sensitive to transaction series length — merchants with <90 days of data produced noisy frequency estimates. We had to impose a minimum observation window, which excluded ~8% of the eligible population. The alternative considered was wavelet decomposition, which handles non-stationary series better but added latency incompatible with real-time decisioning.

Result — Meaningful signal, but with a coverage-accuracy trade-off that constrains applicability to seasoned accounts.
03

Line increment and term loan pilot: guardrails before growth

Product Risk Policy Design

Drafted the pilot framework for line-of-credit increments (up to $1MM) and monthly term loans. The key governance question: how do you size incremental exposure when the existing PD model was calibrated on the original limit distribution? Incrementing limits changes the exposure-at-default distribution, which invalidates the EAD assumptions baked into existing loss forecasts. The pilot was bounded with tighter delinquency guardrails than the base portfolio, and early NPA performance is tracked separately to avoid contaminating base vintage metrics.

Reasoning — Growth products require isolated monitoring — you can't validate incremental risk using the model that didn't see it.
04

Fraud model architecture: GNN vs. traditional features

Liquiloans Model Selection

At Liquiloans, developed a fraud detection model using Graph Neural Networks on KYC and bureau linkage data. The alternative was a feature-engineered gradient boosting approach on flattened relational features. GNN was chosen because fraud patterns in personal lending are inherently relational (shared addresses, phone numbers, employer links) — flattening these into tabular features loses the network structure that distinguishes syndicated fraud from coincidence. Trade-off: GNNs are harder to explain in MRM documentation, and inference latency is higher. The model was deployed as a pre-screen filter rather than an inline decisioning component, mitigating the latency concern.

Outcome — ~₹20Cr fraud mitigation. GNN as pre-screen, not inline — balancing detection power against operational constraints.
Working Principles

Positions held through
production experience.

These aren't axioms — they're conclusions from specific failures and constraints. Open to revision with evidence.

The model is not the decision

A PD model outputs a probability. The decision to approve, decline, or price involves loss appetite, capital allocation, competitive positioning, and regulatory constraints. Conflating model performance (Gini, KS) with portfolio performance (VLR, delinquency rate) is a common error — the model is one input to the decision, not the decision itself.

Bureau data is a floor, not a ceiling

Bureau scores reflect historical repayment behaviour. Transaction-level data captures current liquidity dynamics — approaching cash-flow stress that hasn't yet manifested as a missed payment. Both have limitations: bureau data is backward-looking; transaction data is noisy and vendor-dependent. Neither alone is sufficient for thin-file populations.

Monitoring matters more than initial calibration

A well-calibrated model at deployment will drift. PSI catches distributional shift but not all forms of degradation — concept drift is harder to detect and requires periodic re-estimation, not just threshold alerts. The monitoring framework is part of the model, not an afterthought.

Explainability is a governance requirement, not a virtue signal

SHAP values enable auditability — the ability to trace a specific decline to specific feature contributions for regulatory inquiry. This does not guarantee fairness or correctness. It is the minimum bar for model governance, not a substitute for proper bias testing and adverse action documentation.
